# PM Qualification

## Qualification sequence

1. Extract company, product, use case, current models, token/API usage, spend, users/customers, funding, migration intent, timeline, technical stack, and evidence.
2. Apply hard gates.
3. Estimate projected program-model revenue using the current approved price card.
4. Score quality signals.
5. Assign P0, P1, P2, or Reject.
6. Select the next action and response template.

## Hard gates

- Calculate commercial value from projected program-model usage, not the applicant's current bill from another provider.
- Escalate or reject unauthorized attack payload generation, authentication bypass, exploit automation, or high-risk compliance exposure.
- For legitimate security use cases, confirm authorization scope, asset ownership, payload/PoC boundaries, and disclosure process before offering a pilot.

## Priority tiers

### P0

Clear commercial fit and multiple strong signals. Push a paid pilot or commercial migration promptly.

### P1

Likely fit with one or two important data gaps. Ask focused questions, then move toward a pilot.

### P2

Too early, low-value, vague, or insufficiently evidenced. Provide a minimal API path or nurture; do not push a major pilot.

### Reject

Structural no-fit, prohibited security use, reseller mismatch, or non-commercial use outside program scope.

## Customer routing

- Terminal customer: qualification, ICP check, paid pilot, volume pricing.
- Channel platform: request real program-model consumption before volume pricing; do not promise pilot credits by default.
- VC: qualify portfolio, stage, geography, sectors, and referral intent; do not promise portfolio-wide pricing.
- Reseller or aggregator: do not promise a reseller program; discuss direct or BYOK-style routes only after internal alignment.

## Required questions

- Which models and tools are used today?
- How are they used day to day?
- What is the approximate daily or monthly token/API volume?
- What are the main cost, quality, latency, stability, integration, or compliance issues?
- What should change over the next three to six months?
- For sensitive workloads: what are the data retention, deletion, residency, and compliance requirements?
